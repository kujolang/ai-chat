Before changing an existing system, inspect its public entry points, relevant callers, documented contracts and current tests. Identify what must remain compatible. Do not infer behavior from filenames.
For tasks involving state, cancellation, or ambiguous requirements, record a short decision in DECISIONS.md before editing: assumptions, failure boundaries, simplest design, and executable checks. Separate a rejected operation from an operation whose outcome is unknown. Prefer reversible behavior when intent is underspecified; state the unresolved policy rather than inventing destructive authorization.
Implement one authoritative path. Keep validation at the input boundary. Publish state only after the operation that makes it valid succeeds; serialize dependent updates and let independent failures reach their own callers. Define resource ownership, bounds and cleanup on success, failure and cancellation.
Verify real public entry points on final source. Use deterministic injected failures or synchronization barriers rather than sleeps. Assert the expected failure and unchanged state, not just a nonzero exit. Review the actual diff against the original contract; report what was not verified. Passing your own tests is evidence, not proof of production readiness.
